Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Tucker, GA

The water service line runs from the meter at the street to your house. When it leaks, your bill rises and your yard may get soggy, even though nothing inside the house is wrong. Our 24/7 line sends a plumber to confirm the leak and propose the repair.

Test it: close the shutoff where the line enters the house, then check the meter. If the dial moves, the leak is in the line outside.

The service line is usually the property owner's responsibility from the meter onward, though the exact boundary depends on your utility. DeKalb County Watershed Management handles the water system, and the meter and the street side are theirs. If the leak is at the meter itself, we document and report it.

Our approach is to confirm before digging. We isolate the line, pressure test it and use a ground microphone to locate the leak. A mark on the lawn within a short distance is the goal, not a long trench.

Before anyone digs

Georgia 811 is free, and notice is requested at least three business days before digging so underground utilities are marked. We submit the notice for work we do. Digging without it risks gas, electric and communications lines, and it is the main way service lines get cut by accident. If you are planning your own project near the line, call before you dig. For leaks inside the house, see leak detection.

What to do about a suspected service line leak

  • ✓Close every fixture, read the meter, then close the house main and read it again.
  • ✓Photograph the wet area and the meter reading.
  • ✓Keep vehicles and heavy equipment off the soft ground.
  • ✓Do not dig until the lines are marked.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. Any digging starts with a free call to Georgia 811, at least three business days ahead.
