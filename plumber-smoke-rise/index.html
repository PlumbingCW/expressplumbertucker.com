What Smoke Rise is, and where it sits

Smoke Rise is a long-established residential community in northeastern DeKalb County. The City of Tucker lists several parks that carry its name. Probst Park, on the southwest side of Hugh Howell Road, was formerly called Smoke Rise Park. Smoke Rise Crossing is a wooded passive park once known as Smoke Rise II. Lord Park is a 15.3-acre passive park in the Smoke Rise area. Rosenfeld Park is the former Smoke Rise Bath and Racquet Club and still has a pool. A community association, an elementary school and a country club also use the name.

Boundaries are not published as a single official line, so we describe the area by its anchor streets instead. The city names Hugh Howell Road as the anchor for Probst Park, and Stone Mountain Park lies close by. If you are not sure whether your street counts as Smoke Rise, give the dispatcher your address. What matters for a plumber is the address and the age of the house, not the label.

The houses and what they tell a plumber

Listing sites describe Smoke Rise as an upscale community developed in the 1960s, with brick ranches and split-levels from that era and newer farmhouse-style homes built since. One listing site reports a median year built of 1983 and a median lot of about 20,000 square feet, roughly half an acre. Those are aggregator figures, not a survey of every house, so treat them as a rough picture.

The useful point is the mix. A street can hold a house from the 1960s next to one built in the 2010s, and the two will not share the same plumbing. We never assume what is inside a wall because of the year on a listing. We look, test and tell you what we find. If you know when the house was built or when work was last done, tell the dispatcher, since it helps us bring the right parts.

Trees, lots and the sewer line

Smoke Rise is described as wooded, with mature trees on large lots. That is a pleasant setting, and it is also general plumbing information worth knowing: tree roots look for moisture, and a sewer line with a joint that has loosened can let them in. We do not claim that roots are a common problem in this particular community. We do say that if you have repeat slow drains, a camera look is the fastest way to find out whether roots, a belly in the pipe or ordinary buildup is the cause. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what the footage shows.

Larger lots also mean longer runs of pipe between the street and the house. A longer service line has more joints, and a leak under a lawn can go unnoticed for weeks. Watch the meter. If it keeps moving with every fixture off, call and describe it.

Local note: the address you see may say Stone Mountain

Listings for Smoke Rise streets can show a Stone Mountain or a Tucker mailing address, and ZIP code 30087 appears on them. That ZIP is also used for parts of the city of Stone Mountain, so a ZIP code alone does not tell you which city you are in. For plumbing, the useful question is whether your water and sewer come from DeKalb County Watershed Management, which is the case for addresses in DeKalb County that use the public system.

If your home is not on the public sewer, tell the dispatcher at the start of the call. A septic system calls for different work, and we would rather know before we plan the visit.

When water heaters are the issue

In houses that have stood for decades, the water heater has often been replaced once or twice. A tank typically lasts around a decade, though it varies with water quality and maintenance. Signs it is near the end include rusty hot water, a rumbling tank and moisture at the base. Read about water heater replacement before you commit, and ask any plumber to put the venting and code items on the quote.
