Choosing the unit before the install day

A planned installation lets you compare options at your own pace. The main choices are fuel type, tank size and efficiency level. Gas units heat faster and run cheaper in many homes, but need a flue and a gas line. Electric units are simpler to install and available in compact models. Hybrid heat pump units use less electricity but need airflow and a drain.

We walk the install location, check the existing connections, and recommend a model that fits the space and your household's habits. You decide. We install.

Safe temperature and the setting most homes should use

Most manufacturers ship tanks with a thermostat set high. Setting the water temperature to about 120 degrees Fahrenheit reduces scald risk, especially in homes with young children or older adults. If anyone in the house has a weakened immune system, ask us about the trade-offs, because very low settings can encourage bacterial growth in a tank.

Local notes for Tucker installs

Drinking water in Tucker comes through DeKalb County Watershed Management. The utility's annual report does not state hardness, so test your tap if you want to know whether a scale-resistant model or a regular flush schedule matters in your house.

Where a heater sits in a closet, clearance is the usual problem. We check clearances before we order, because a heater that fits on paper can fail the access test once the door is open. For homes in Stone Mountain or Clarkston, the same planning applies.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. Gas piping and venting for water heaters fall under the National Fuel Gas Code, NFPA 54, as adopted locally.
