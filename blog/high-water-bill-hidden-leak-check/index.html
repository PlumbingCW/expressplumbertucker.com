High Water Bill? A Ten-Minute Meter Test for a Hidden Leak

A bill that climbs without a change in your habits is a clue. Water is leaving the system somewhere, and you can find out whether the leak is inside the house or outside it with nothing but your meter and ten minutes.

This test does not fix anything, but it tells you what to ask the plumber for.

The meter test

1

Stop all water use

Close faucets, make sure the dishwasher and washer are off and no one is showering.

2

Find the meter

It is usually in a box near the street. Lift the lid carefully.

3

Read the indicator

Look for the small triangle or dial that spins when water flows.

4

Wait ten minutes

Note whether the dial moves. If it does, water is escaping.

5

Close the house main

Check again. If the dial stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the line outside.

Write down the reading and the time. A second reading in an hour tells you how fast the leak is running.

The toilet is the usual suspect

A toilet can run silently and waste a great deal of water. Drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank and wait twenty minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. It is an inexpensive part and the most common cause of a surprise bill.

When the leak is hidden

A leak in a wall, ceiling or slab does not announce itself. The signs are a warm floor spot, damp drywall, a musty smell or the sound of running water in a quiet house. Electronic equipment pinpoints the spot so the repair opening is small.

If the leak is under the floor slab, see slab leak repair for what the options are before anyone breaks concrete.

Before you call about a bill

  • ✓Do the meter test and note the result.
  • ✓Check the last few months of bills for a pattern.
  • ✓Photograph the meter and any damp areas.
  • ✓Ask the utility whether your meter reads in gallons or cubic feet.

Talk to the utility too

If the meter moves with your house main closed, the leak is between the meter and the house, and you are generally responsible for it. If the meter itself looks damaged or is leaking at the box, call the utility. We help you decide which side the problem is on.

Reading your bill like a plumber

Look at the usage line, not just the total. Compare this month to the same month last year, since summer irrigation changes use. A steady climb over several months suggests a slow leak. A sudden jump suggests a break or a failed part such as a stuck fill valve.

If the bill rose after a repair or a change in the household, check what changed. A new guest, a filled pool or a new irrigation zone can all explain a jump. If nothing changed, the meter test is the next step.

The toilet, the heater and the irrigation line

Three places account for most house leaks that are not in the walls. The toilet leaks through the flapper. The water heater leaks at a fitting or the relief valve. The irrigation system leaks underground, where you cannot see it. Check each before suspecting a pipe in the wall.

What a plumber does with a positive meter test

1

Isolate the zone

Fixture shutoffs and the house main are used to narrow where the leak sits.

2

Pressure test

The supply system is tested to see whether it holds pressure.

3

Listen and scan

Acoustic equipment and thermal imaging narrow the location.

4

Mark the spot

The leak is marked on the surface so the opening is as small as possible.

5

Propose repair

You are given the repair options and what each involves.

You do not need to guess. Giving the plumber the meter result and the bill history saves time on site. If the meter is moving and you cannot find the cause, a detection visit is faster and cheaper than opening walls on a hunch.

Keep the evidence

Photograph the meter, save the bills and keep the plumber's report. If the leak caused damage, these documents support an insurance claim. Some utilities will consider an adjustment for a verified leak, and policies differ, so ask yours.

When it is not a leak

Not every high bill is a leak. Seasonal irrigation, a new baby, a visiting family and a long hot spell all raise use. Check whether the bill reflects a rate change or a different billing period. If the meter does not move with everything off, the usage is real, and the answer is habit, not repair.

Sources and standards

Meter reading steps vary by model, so check your utility's guidance. Repairs follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Georgia.

Local note: where the water meter sits

In Tucker and nearby DeKalb towns like Stone Mountain and Clarkston, the meter is read by DeKalb County Watershed Management. The meter and the street side belong to the utility, while the line from the meter to your house is generally yours.

Dial layouts vary. If yours is unclear, take a photo and show it to the dispatcher.
