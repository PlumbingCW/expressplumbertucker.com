Tankless Water Heater Service in Tucker, GA

Tankless heaters heat water as it flows, so there is no tank to run out. When one stops working, the symptoms are different from a tank: error codes, lukewarm bursts, or a unit that shuts off mid-shower.

This page covers installation, repair and the yearly flush that keeps these units running.

My tankless heater shows an error code or gives cold bursts

An error code is the unit telling you which sensor or system tripped. Common causes include a blocked intake or exhaust vent, scale on the heat exchanger, a failed flow sensor or ignition problems on gas models. A cold burst in the middle of a hot shower often means the flow dipped below the level the unit needs to stay lit.

We read the code, check gas pressure or electrical supply, inspect the venting and test flow. Many faults are cleaned, adjusted or reset on the first visit. If the heat exchanger itself has failed, we explain what the manufacturer's warranty may cover.

Descaling and flush: why it matters

Minerals in the water coat the inside of the heat exchanger over time. The coating acts as insulation, so the unit works harder for less heat and eventually trips. A flush uses a mild acid solution circulated through the unit, followed by a clean rinse. It restores flow and often clears error codes.

Tucker's supplier, DeKalb County Watershed Management, does not list a hardness figure in its annual report, so we suggest testing your tap. Whatever the number, an annual flush is cheap insurance on a unit that costs several times more than a tank heater.

Is a tankless heater right for your house?

Tankless units suit homes where space is tight, usage is steady and the gas line or electrical service can carry the demand. Gas models typically need a larger gas line than a tank heater and a vent rated for the unit. Electric tankless models draw heavy current, and some panels cannot support them.

If you are considering a swap from a tank, we visit, check the gas line size and the venting path, and give you a plain recommendation. Some households do better with a new tank. See water heater replacement or planned water heater installation for that route.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. Gas piping and venting for water heaters fall under the National Fuel Gas Code, NFPA 54, as adopted locally.
