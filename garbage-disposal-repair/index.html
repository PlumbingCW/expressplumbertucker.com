Garbage Disposal Repair in Tucker, GA

Humming, silent or leaking: what each one means

SignLikely problemFix
Hums, will not spinJam in the grinding ringFree the jam, reset the unit
Silent, no humTripped reset or dead motor or switchReset, test power, then diagnose
Leak at the top flangeFailed sink sealReseat the flange with new putty
Leak from the sideLoose drain or dishwasher hoseTighten or replace the hose
Leak from the bottomCracked housing or failed internal sealReplace the unit

A jam is common after something hard or fibrous lands in the chamber, such as a bone, fruit pit or too much stringy vegetable. The motor tries to turn and trips. There is a hex socket in the bottom of most units that lets us turn the plate by hand and release it, and a reset button on the base that restores power.

A leak at the bottom is different. Water in the housing corrodes the motor and shaft. That unit is done, and paying for a patch is not good value.

Clogged sink or slow drainage

A disposal that grinds but the sink drains slowly usually has a clog in the trap or the line beyond it. The unit only chops, it does not carry waste to the main. Grease and starch settle in the drain and build into a plug. If a sink backs up and the disposal is clean, the next step is drain cleaning.

What to do and not do

  • ✓Switch it off at the wall or breaker before checking anything.
  • ✓Run cold water while grinding and for 15 seconds after.
  • ✓Keep grease, coffee grounds, eggshells in bulk, pasta and rice out of the unit.
  • ✓Never use chemical drain cleaners in a sink with a disposal.

Replacing a disposal

A new unit makes sense when the housing leaks, the motor is dead, or the unit is old and noisy. Horsepower, feed type and sound insulation all affect price and performance. We can install a unit you bought, or supply one, connect the drain and dishwasher line, and wire it to the existing outlet. If new wiring or a new circuit is needed, that is electrical work, and we tell you plainly when an electrician is the right call.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. When you shop for toilets and faucets, the EPA WaterSense label marks models that meet its water efficiency criteria, and WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less.
