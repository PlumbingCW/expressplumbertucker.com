Plumbing calls from Stone Mountain homes

Stone Mountain is a DeKalb County city of about 6,703 residents, and its streets run from the older village center to subdivisions that were built decades apart. A house near Main Street and the Village has a different plumbing history than a newer home farther out, and that history decides what fails first.

The calls we see most are backed-up drains, water heaters that leak or quit, running toilets and pipes that burst after a cold night. For a clog that returns, drain cleaning or hydro jetting is usually the answer. For hot water, see water heater repair.

Older houses, older pipes

Stone Mountain's story starts early. The railroad arrived in 1845 and the town grew around granite and the depot. Old neighborhoods often still carry old supply lines and cast iron drains, which corrode from the inside. If you have rusty water, falling pressure or repeated pinhole leaks, ask us about repiping before another patch. We assess first and tell you honestly if a repair is enough.

Local note: what the Village area teaches

The Stone Mountain Village around Main Street holds some of the oldest buildings in the city, including the Wells-Brown House. Older structures like these are where we most often find original lines and unusual fittings, so we look before we connect anything new.

If you own an older building near the Village, tell the dispatcher its approximate age. That helps us arrive with the right parts.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. Water and sewer service is provided by the county utility for your address, and we confirm which one applies.
