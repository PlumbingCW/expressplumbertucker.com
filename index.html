What counts as a plumbing emergency

Not every plumbing problem needs a midnight visit, and an honest emergency plumber tells you which ones can wait. These cannot:

  • Water spraying or pouring from a pipe, a ceiling or a water heater. Close the main shutoff and call. See burst pipe repair.
  • Sewage coming up in tubs, showers or floor drains. Stop using water and read about sewer line cleaning.
  • No hot water in a house with children, older adults or a business that cannot open. Start with water heater repair.
  • A gas smell near a water heater. Leave the house, call the gas utility from outside, then call us.
  • A meter that keeps spinning with every fixture off. This is the sign of a hidden leak, covered under leak detection.

A dripping faucet, a slow drain in one sink or a toilet that runs can usually wait for a daytime visit. If you are unsure, call and describe it. The dispatcher tells you what to expect.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Permit rules are local, so ask the City of Tucker permit team about your address. Georgia licenses journeyman and master plumbers through the Secretary of State, and any plumber you hire should give you a license number you can verify.
