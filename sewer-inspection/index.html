When a camera look is worth it

Buying a house

The sewer line is a major hidden system. A camera look before closing can reveal roots, breaks or a belly.

Repeat clogs

If a line clogs again and again, the camera shows why before you pay for more cleaning.

Slow drains throughout

A camera tells you whether the line is narrowed by buildup or damaged.

Odors or wet lawn patches

A leaking sewer can show up as smell or soft ground. A camera can find the leak path.

How the inspection works

1

Locate the access

We find a cleanout or a removable toilet access point.

2

Run the camera

A flexible rod carries a waterproof camera along the pipe while we record.

3

Mark the problems

We note footage distance for roots, cracks, offsets, bellies or scale.

4

Explain findings

You see the video and hear what each finding means for repair or maintenance.

The camera head shows a live picture on a monitor and records video. A locator at the surface can tell us where the camera is, so a problem 40 feet down can be pinpointed in the yard. That matters when a spot repair is the right answer.

Findings do not oblige you to repair anything. A minor offset on a line that drains well may only need monitoring. A cracked section under the driveway deserves a plan.

For buyers: ask before the inspection window closes

A general home inspection usually does not include a sewer scope. If you are buying, ask your agent whether your contract allows time for one. A recorded camera pass gives you leverage in negotiation if the line is damaged and a clean bill of health if it is not. Send us the address and the access details and we will tell you what is possible.

Inspecting lines in Tucker and nearby towns

Tucker homes sit in the DeKalb County sewer system, and the county handles the mains while you handle your side line. We inspect private laterals in Tucker, Clarkston, Stone Mountain, Lilburn and Norcross. If an inspection finds a problem past your property line, we report it to the utility with the footage so you are not paying for a public fix. For repair of damage found, see sewer line repair.

What happens to the footage

You receive the video and a short written summary the same day. Keep both. If you later sell the house, a clean recent scope is a selling point. If you are in a dispute about a line that crosses a property boundary, the video with distance markers is evidence.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number.
