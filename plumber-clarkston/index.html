What plumbing looks like in a dense city

Clarkston has 5,398 housing units, and a large share of them are shared buildings. When one unit's drain backs up, neighbors often follow, and the cause is a shared line rather than a single toilet. That changes who to call and what to do first.

Tenants should tell the property manager and keep the plumber's number handy. Landlords and managers can call us for common-line problems, and we describe findings in plain terms. See commercial plumbing for multi-unit buildings and small businesses.

Building types and what fails

Clarkston was incorporated in 1882, and its building stock spans decades. Older low-rise apartment buildings tend to have shared drain stacks and cast iron that narrows with age. Newer construction has plastic lines that fail at fittings instead. We identify the material before choosing a repair, and a camera look tells us whether a line needs cleaning or something more. Read about sewer line cleaning for how that works.

Local note: the divide beneath the street

Clarkston sits at about 1,020 feet and lies along the Eastern Continental Divide, the ridge that sends water toward the Atlantic on one side and the Gulf on the other. In practice, that means ground that drains in several directions and lines that follow the slope.

For a plumber, the point is simple: a sewer line on a slope runs faster, and low points collect debris. We look at the line profile on the camera before deciding where to clean or repair.

Schools and campuses nearby

The Perimeter College campus sits just south of the city, and the Atlanta Area School for the Deaf is also in Clarkston. Institutions have their own facilities teams, and we work with them when they call for outside help. For restroom clogs, water heater swaps or leak tracing in any building near the campuses, the same 24/7 number applies.

ZIP code and utility

Clarkston uses ZIP code 30021. It lies in DeKalb County, so water and sewer service is handled by DeKalb County Watershed Management. When a problem might be on the public side, we document it with a meter test or camera, and tell you who to call. See also sewer line repair for what happens when a camera finds damage.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. Water and sewer service is provided by the county utility for your address, and we confirm which one applies.
