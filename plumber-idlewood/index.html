Plumber in Idlewood, Tucker, GA

Idlewood is a group of residential subdivisions in Tucker around Idlewood Road. Neighbors there stay in touch through an informal group, and the houses range across several decades of building.

This page is for Idlewood-area homeowners who want a plumber, or who want to understand what to expect from the plumbing in a house of a certain age.

Which streets and subdivisions count as Idlewood

The Idlewood Coalition of Neighbors, known as ICON, is an unofficial neighbor group listed by the Tucker Civic Association. It was started about fifteen years ago and has no officers. Its listing names the subdivisions it covers: Drayton Woods, Harbour Oaks, Idlewood Acres East and West, Cedar Knoll, Idlewood Parc, Classic Village and Browning Chase, plus some neighbors on Idlewood Road itself. Idlewood Elementary, part of the DeKalb County School District, also uses the name.

No formal boundary is published, so we describe the area by this list and by Idlewood Road. If your street is in one of those subdivisions or on the road, you are in what locals call Idlewood. If you are not sure, give the dispatcher your address.

What the housing data suggest, and what they do not

Public listing data give a rough picture. A neighborhood-statistics site describes many Idlewood-area homes as built between 1940 and 1969, with a number from 1970 to 1999, and says most residences are owner-occupied. Sales data for individual subdivisions show a median year built of about 1969 for Drayton Woods and 1982 for Classic Village. These are aggregator figures drawn from recent sales, not an official count.

The practical meaning is that one street can contain houses from different decades, each with its own pipes, fixtures and water heater history. We do not assume galvanized pipe or old cast iron because of a date. We check. If a camera or a pressure test shows a problem, we explain it and the options.

Houses from the 1960s

Original fixtures may have been replaced already, so ask what has been done. Look under sinks and at the main shutoff for signs of age, corrosion or past repairs.

Houses from the 1970s and 1980s

Water heaters, toilets and faucets are the usual replacement items by now. Check supply lines for bulging or stains and replace worn shutoff valves.

Houses from the 1990s on

Plumbing here is newer, and issues tend to be at fittings, fixtures and water heaters rather than whole systems. Regular checks are the best prevention.

Remodeled houses

Additions and kitchen or bath projects can mix old and new materials. Ask who did the work and whether it was permitted.

Common calls from houses of this kind

  • Water heater that is a decade or more old and leaking, rumbling or running out of hot water.
  • Toilets that run, rock or leave a wet floor from a worn seal.
  • Faucets and shutoff valves that drip, stick or will not close fully.
  • Hose bibs that leak inside the wall after a freeze.
  • Slow drains that return after being cleared.

For a water heater near the end of its life, start with water heater repair to see whether a fix makes sense, or read about replacement. For fixtures, see toilet repair and faucet repair. Drains that keep coming back are covered under drain cleaning.

Questions to ask any plumber about an older house

1

Ask what they will check

A good answer includes the main shutoff, pressure, visible pipes and the water heater.

2

Ask for the finding in plain words

They should be able to say what failed, why and what the options are.

3

Ask what is permitted

Some work needs a permit. The City of Tucker handles permits for addresses inside the city.

4

Ask about warranty

Get the terms for parts and labor in writing.

5

Keep the paperwork

Photos, invoices and any inspection record help when you sell or file an insurance claim.

Local note: a neighbor group, not a city line

ICON describes itself as informal, and the Idlewood name covers several subdivisions without a published boundary. That is common in older parts of metro Atlanta. For plumbing it means one thing: the useful fact is your street address, not the label. Tell the dispatcher the street and what you see.

Water and sewer service for DeKalb County addresses on the public system comes from DeKalb County Watershed Management. If a leak might be on the utility side of the meter, we document it and tell you whom to call.
