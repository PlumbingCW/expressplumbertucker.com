Installation is where small shortcuts become leaks later. A supply line over-tightened at the stop, a toilet set on an uneven floor, a drain with too little slope. We test each connection under pressure and run water before we leave, so the first leak is found by us, not by you.

We also tell you what we find. If a stop valve is seized, a trap is corroded or a flange is cracked, we explain what it means for the new fixture and quote the extra work separately.

Remodels and additions

A kitchen or bathroom remodel needs plumbing at two moments: rough-in before the walls close and final connection after. We coordinate with your contractor on fixture locations and the schedule. If you are adding a bathroom, new drain and vent lines and supply runs are part of the scope, and we plan them so access remains if a part ever needs service.

Permits and inspections

Plumbing work may require a permit depending on its scope, and rules vary by address. The City of Tucker permit team can tell you what applies, and we describe the planned work so the question is specific. Tucker's Building and Permitting office inspects at stages, while the county handles water and sewer connections. When you call 811 before digging, Georgia law asks for at least three business days of notice.

Installing in Tucker homes

Tucker's mix of older ranches and newer townhomes means we meet different plumbing on each visit. Older houses may have galvanized or cast iron lines behind a new fixture, and we check before connecting. If the old supply lines are failing, we raise whole-house repiping as an honest option, and for new outdoor or irrigation connections we talk through the service line first.

After the install: care and warranty

Ask for the fixture manual and the warranty card, and keep them with your records. Run each new fixture for a minute and check the supply connections under the sink a day later, once the parts have settled. If something drips or sounds wrong, call and we return to check it. Small adjustments after an install are normal and expected.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. When you shop for toilets and faucets, the EPA WaterSense label marks models that meet its water efficiency criteria, and WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less.
