Whole-House Repiping in Tucker, GA

If your water runs brown, your pressure fades, or pinhole leaks keep appearing, the pipes may be wearing out from the inside. We assess before we recommend, and repiping is only one of the options.

A single leak does not always mean replacement. Several leaks in different spots usually does.

Rusty water, low pressure, or leaks that keep coming back

Brown or orange water

Corroding galvanized or iron pipe sheds rust into the flow.

Pressure that fades over years

Mineral and rust buildup narrows the pipe from inside.

Pinhole leaks in different places

Pinholes in copper or galvanized pipe mean the wall is thinning along the run.

Stains on walls or ceilings

Slow leaks in concealed pipes. See leak detection to trace them.

Plumbers see the same pattern: a repair here, another leak there, and eventually the repairs cost more than the pipe is worth. The point where you stop patching is the point to ask about repiping.

We start by looking. A pressure test, a camera in accessible lines and a review of where past leaks occurred tell us whether the problem is local or systemic.

PEX or copper

PEX is flexible, needs fewer fittings and goes through walls with less demolition. Copper has a long record and tolerates heat and sunlight. We explain the trade-offs, including how each handles your water and your house, and let you decide. Either one is a substantial improvement over a failing system, and either is installed to the plumbing code that applies.

How a repipe works

1

Assess and plan

We inspect, test pressure and map the run of the new lines.

2

Protect the house

We lay drop cloths and plan the openings to minimize patching.

3

Install new lines

New supply lines go in, connected to fixtures and the water heater.

4

Test and inspect

We pressure test and arrange any required inspection.

5

Patch and finish

Openings are closed, and we explain what drywall finishing remains.

Repiping typically takes days, not weeks, and we plan the order so the house is without water for as short a time as possible. Permits may apply, so ask the City of Tucker permit team about your address, and we describe the scope so the question is precise.

What affects repiping cost

House size

More fixtures and longer runs cost more.

Material

PEX and copper are in different price classes.

Access

Open crawlspaces help, finished slab homes are harder.

Drywall repair

Patching and painting may be separate from plumbing.

Permits and inspection

Required inspections add scheduling.

Repiping in Tucker houses

Tucker's housing includes decades-old neighborhoods built long before PEX existed, so many houses still carry their original supply lines. We do not guess about your pipes: we open an access point, identify the material and count the failures. Related repair paths include burst pipe repair for sudden failures and water service line repair when the problem is between the meter and the house.

Living in the house during a repipe

Plan for a few days with interruptions to water service. We tell you in advance which hours the water will be off, and we restore service to a few fixtures overnight where we can. Keep a supply of drinking water, plan showers elsewhere on the busiest days and move furniture away from the walls we will open. Dust protection is part of the plan.

Standards and sources

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the state minimum plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask the City of Tucker permit team which edition and which local rules apply to your address. Plumbers are licensed through the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and you can ask for a license number. Any digging starts with a free call to Georgia 811, at least three business days ahead.
